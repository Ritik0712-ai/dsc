# Task 1: Product Thinking

## App Chosen: Instagram

Instagram is one of the most-used apps in the world, and I use it
daily — primarily for Reels, Stories, and chatting with friends.
As someone deeply embedded in the platform, I've noticed patterns
that genuinely frustrate me as a user. If I were part of Instagram's
product team, here are the changes I would make.

---

## Feature I Would Add: Feed Control (Algorithm Transparency & Reset)

### The Problem

Instagram's algorithm is one of the best in the world at keeping
you engaged — but that's also its biggest flaw. Once you start
watching a particular genre of Reels, say FIFA World Cup highlights,
the algorithm locks you in. It assumes that's all you ever want to
see, and your entire feed gets dominated by that one category. You
get cut off from everything else happening around the world.

This is good for short-term engagement but bad for the user
experience in the long run. I've personally felt trapped in content
loops where I wanted to explore something new, but the algorithm
just wouldn't let go of what I was watching last week.

### The Feature

I would introduce **Feed Control** — a simple settings panel where
users can:

- Select the content categories or genres they want to see
  (e.g., Sports, Tech, Comedy, Travel, Finance)
- Adjust the weightage of each category using a slider
- Reset their algorithm with one tap, essentially giving themselves
  a "fresh feed" without losing their follow list

This gives the user agency over their own experience. Right now,
the only way to "fix" your algorithm is to consciously like and
engage with different content for weeks — which is frustrating and
unintuitive. Feed Control makes this process instant and deliberate.

### Why It Matters

YouTube has already implemented something similar with its
interest controls. Spotify lets you build taste profiles. Instagram
is behind on this. Users increasingly want to feel in control of
what they consume, not just be passive recipients of whatever the
algorithm decides. Feed Control directly addresses this.

---

## Feature I Would Add: Close Friends Feed

### The Problem

Instagram already has a "Close Friends" list — but it's only used
for sharing Stories privately. The feature stops there, which feels
like a massive missed opportunity.

### The Feature

I would introduce a **Close Friends Feed** — a dedicated tab or
section where you can see all the Reels, Stories, and posts that
your close friends have recently liked, commented on, or watched.

Think of it as a social activity layer specifically for your inner
circle. Instead of just seeing what Instagram thinks you want, you
get a window into what the people you actually care about are
engaging with right now.

This keeps you socially in sync with your peers in a natural,
non-intrusive way — you don't need to text someone "hey what have
you been watching lately?" You just check your Close Friends Feed.

### Why It Matters

Spotify has Friend Activity on its desktop app — you can see what
your friends are listening to in real time. It drives organic
discovery and deepens social connection on the platform. Instagram
could achieve the same thing with this feature, especially since
the Close Friends infrastructure already exists.

---

## Feature I Would Tweak: Home Feed Priority

### The Problem

Currently, Instagram's home feed mixes content from accounts you
follow with suggested posts from strangers. The balance is off —
sometimes you scroll for minutes before seeing a post from someone
you actually chose to follow.

### The Tweak

I wouldn't remove stranger suggestions — they serve a purpose for
content discovery and are important for Instagram's growth engine.
But I would **deprioritize** them. If I follow 300 accounts, the
majority of my feed should reflect those 300 accounts. Stranger
content should be secondary — a supplement, not the main course.

A simple toggle like "Prioritize Following" would solve this
without breaking the existing recommendation system.

---

## Feature I Would Add: Reels Watch History

### The Problem

We watch hundreds of Reels every single day. Sometimes you see a
Reel that genuinely interests you but you forget to save or like
it in the moment. A day later, you want to find it again — and
there's no way to. It's just gone.

YouTube has already solved this exact problem with YouTube Shorts
— it maintains a full watch history just like regular YouTube
videos. Instagram has no equivalent.

### The Feature

A **Reels Watch History** — accessible from your profile, showing
the last N Reels you've watched, sorted by date. This isn't just
a convenience feature. It also helps users become more aware of
their own consumption patterns, which ties directly back to the
Feed Control idea.

### Why It Matters

Yes, users watch hundreds of Reels per day — but that's exactly
why history matters more, not less. The volume makes it impossible
to rely on memory. YouTube proved this is technically feasible even
at scale. There's no good reason Instagram hasn't built it.

---

## Summary

All four of these changes — Feed Control, Close Friends Feed, Home
Feed Priority, and Watch History — point to the same core insight:

> Instagram optimizes for the platform's engagement metrics.
> These features would shift that balance toward optimizing for
> the user's actual experience.

That's the product philosophy I'd bring to Instagram's team.
